1. Agile Team Charter — Reflection

My role in the RideConnect project was recorded as Wireframes Designer. The charter helped define responsibilities and gave our team a basis for coordinating different parts of the project. In the group chat, I shared Figma wireframes on 23 May 2026 and asked for feedback. This illustrates how my design work depended on communication with other team members rather than working in isolation. I learned that agreeing on roles is only the starting point: we also need to keep each other informed, review work together, and clarify how design decisions affect the rest of the application.

2. User Stories and Tasks — Reflection

Wireframing helped me think about the RideConnect application from the perspective of its users. The project includes driver and rider interactions, such as registration, ride offers, requests and booking-related screens. By creating and sharing wireframes, I contributed to discussing how users might move through the application before all features were implemented. The group discussion about driver and passenger views shows why it was important to distinguish the needs of these two user types. I learned that a screen should be designed around a clear user goal, not simply around its appearance. The exact user stories I personally wrote or edited still need to be checked against Trello.

3. Product Backlog — Reflection

The team used Trello to organize the RideConnect backlog, and the group circulated an updated board for review on 30 July 2026. From a design perspective, I could use the proposed features to understand which screens and interactions needed attention. Sharing wireframes made it easier for the team to discuss how planned features might work in practice. I learned that backlog items and interface designs should be aligned: if a feature is unclear, the design can expose questions that the team needs to resolve. I still need to verify whether I personally changed any backlog priorities before making that claim.

4. Sprint Backlog — Reflection

On 23 July 2026, I volunteered for Sprint 3, which the group identified as UX Design. The team also discussed other sprint areas, including planning, user journeys, backend, frontend and testing. This showed me how UX design fits into a larger sequence of collaborative work. I learned that sprint planning should turn broad goals into specific tasks that teammates can understand and review. My documented responsibility was UX Design, but the exact tasks completed and sprint dates must be confirmed from the team's Trello records.
