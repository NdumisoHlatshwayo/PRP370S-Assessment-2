Individual reflection

The dated messages helped me distinguish between a planned meeting, a recorded decision and a contribution I can personally demonstrate. On 20 April I asked what to prepare, on 23 May I shared wireframes for feedback, and on 23 July I volunteered for UX Design. These records show why an Agile logbook should capture actions and follow-up outcomes rather than only meeting invitations.

A challenge was that communication and task updates were spread across chat messages and Trello. This made it harder to connect design feedback with specific sprint tasks. In future, I would record decisions, task owners, links to artefacts, and review outcomes immediately after each session. I would also distinguish work in progress from completed and tested deliverables.

Individual reflection / WhatsApp group evidence

What working with the RideConnect team taught me

Working on RideConnect helped me understand that Agile teamwork depends on clear communication, shared responsibility and regular feedback, not simply dividing tasks between group members. The WhatsApp messages show that the team used Microsoft Teams links and meeting reminders to coordinate work in April, discussed how to organise meetings, shared design resources, and later circulated the Trello backlog. This gave us ways to stay connected, but the conversation also showed how easily plans and updates can become scattered across different channels.

From my own messages, I can point to specific moments where I tried to participate constructively. On 20 April 2026, I asked what I should prepare, showing that I wanted to understand expectations before contributing. On 23 May 2026, I shared Figma wireframes and requested feedback, which helped me appreciate that a design improves when other people can question and review it. On 23 July 2026, I volunteered for the UX Design work area when the team discussed sprint responsibilities. Those are documented contributions rather than claims that I personally completed every group task.

One difficulty was keeping track of decisions and progress. Some messages asked whether meetings were taking place, while another update indicated that a teammate would brief me after a meeting. This reminded me that not everyone always has the same information at the same time. I learned that asking questions early, documenting decisions, and following up on feedback are important for avoiding misunderstandings and duplicated effort.

Overall, I value the experience because it strengthened my confidence in sharing design work, communicating with teammates and taking responsibility for a defined contribution. If I worked on a similar project again, I would suggest a short shared meeting summary after each discussion, clearer task owners and acceptance criteria on Trello, and a simple record of what feedback was received and what changed as a result. That would make collaboration more transparent and help us evaluate our progress more fairly.
