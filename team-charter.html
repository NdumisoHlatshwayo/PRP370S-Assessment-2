Agile Team Charter

The project's purpose, team responsibilities and working agreements, connected to dated evidence of my UX design involvement.

Project vision & scope

Documented team plan

RideConnect was proposed as a carpooling mobile application and website for students and workers commuting in Cape Town. Planned features included account registration, driver ride offers, passenger search and joining, trip details, and avoiding seat overbooking.

These are requirements described by the charter, not independently verified completed features.

Roles and team agreements

My responsibility

Wireframes Designer

Design key screen layouts and help plan a clear experience for both riders and drivers. My Figma wireframe sharing and UX Design volunteering provide dated supporting evidence of design involvement.

Ways of working

Communication and ceremonies

The charter describes Microsoft Teams communication, approximately 30-minute meetings around 8 PM, stand-ups, sprint planning, reviews, retrospectives, shared decisions and respectful feedback.

These were agreed intentions, not proof every ceremony occurred.

Individual reflection

The charter helped me understand that my responsibility as Wireframes Designer was connected to the wider team's planning, rather than being an isolated design activity. Sharing wireframes for feedback on 23 May 2026 made it possible for other members to comment on how passengers and drivers would move between screens. When I volunteered for UX Design during the July sprint discussion, I could see how early interface planning connected to development tasks.

I learned that an Agile charter is most useful when its agreements are followed by clear communication and visible work. In future, I would make feedback requests more specific and record design decisions and their outcomes directly on the task board. I cannot confirm from the available records that every planned meeting or retrospective occurred, and I do not claim to have written the entire team charter.
